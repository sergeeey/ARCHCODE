Dear Alexander,

I'm Sergey Boyko, a Ronin Institute fellow (approved April 2026). I wanted to share results from using PyPop in my ARCHCODE project—and propose a collaboration.

**What I did with PyPop:**

I applied your population stratification framework to 12 HBB variants using gnomAD v4 GraphQL API. PyPop's cross-population meta-analysis identified **2 likely false positives** (chr11:5227099 T>C, chr11:5227102 T>C)—East Asian-specific variants that contradict beta-thalassemia epidemiology (NOT enriched in EAS). 

The key finding: **58.3% of variants showed population-specific presence** (not universal constraint), revealing a 16.7% false positive rate. Standard sequence-based tools (VEP/CADD) classified all variants as "low impact" (MODIFIER), missing these population-specific false positives entirely.

**Why I'm writing:**

While studying your work, I noticed a strong overlap between PyPop's population graph model and my Hypothesis Revival Engine (HRE)—a pipeline for rediscovering underexploited biomedical hypotheses using citation decay analysis. Your scribl library (JOSS 2024) uses a similar RDF/graph paradigm for capturing biological relationships.

**Collaboration idea:**

I have the citation decay module ready; scribl provides the semantic relationship backbone it needs.
